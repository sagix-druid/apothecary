In 1893 the U.S. Treasury was paying out gold against silver-backed notes. President Cleveland traced the drain to the compromise Congress passed in 1890.

Between July 1890 and July 1893 the gold in the Treasury fell by more than $132 million while its silver rose by more than $147 million. In 1895 the Treasury sold bonds for gold to a syndicate headed by J. P. Morgan.
