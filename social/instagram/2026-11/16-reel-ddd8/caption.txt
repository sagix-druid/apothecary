In 1990 Brazil's annual inflation peaked at 2,947.7 percent, and in March of that year prices rose 81.3 percent in a single month.

The root was institutional: state banks, Banco do Brasil and the Central Bank could all create money, and each one undermined the others. Six stabilization plans failed before the Real Plan worked in 1994.
